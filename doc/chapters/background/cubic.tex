\section{27 lines on a cubic surface}
From \cref{cor:Fano}, we know that a generic smooth cubic surface contains finitely many lines. We will now show that it contains exactly 27 lines. The following is the main theorem of this section.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:27}
    Each smooth cubic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ contains exactly 27 distinct lines.
\end{theorem}

We will prove it in two different ways. The first will relate cubic surfaces to blow-ups of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in 6 points, allowing us to picture the lines on smooth cubic surfaces concretely, aiding us later. The second result will use the degree of certain Chern classes to compute the degree of the Fano scheme of lines on a cubic surface, giving us the number of lines directly.

This proof will rely on theory which is not in the scope of this thesis, but it will allow us to draw a picture to show a representation of the 27 lines themselves, which we will use later.

First, the points $P_1, \dots, P_6$ in $\mathbb{P}^2$ are said to be in \emph{general position} if no three of the points are collinear, and no six lie on a conic. 

\begin{proposition}[\cite{Ha77}, 4.7/4.7.3]\label{prop:blowup6}
    Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a smooth cubic surface. Then there exist points $P_1, \dots, P_6 \in \mathbb{P}^2$ in general position such that $X$ is isomorphic to the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in these points.
\end{proposition}
This is part of a bigger class of surfaces, those being the \emph{Del Pezzo} surfaces. We refer to \cite[Chapter 4]{BEA96} for more information on these.

\begin{proposition}[\cite{Ha77}, Theorem V.4.9]\label{prop:27}
    () Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a smooth cubic surface. Then $X$ contains exactly 27 lines. Each has self-intersection $-1$, and they are the only irreducible curves with negative self-intersection. They are:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The exceptional curves $E_1, \dots E_6$ (6 of these)
        \item The strict transform of lines in $\mathbb{P}^2$ containing pairs of points $P_i, P_j$, $i \neq j$, denoted $\ell_{ij}$ (15 of these).
        \item The strict transform of conics in $\mathbb{P}^2$ not containing $P_i$ denoted $C_i$ (6 of these).
    \end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Proof by degree of Chern classes}
Recall from Example \ref{ex:G13} that $\mathbb{G}(1,3)$ has dimension 4.
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:27}.]
    Given a smooth cubic surface $X \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ determined by the vanishing of a general section $g \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(3)$, we wish to determine the degree of the locus in $\mathbb{G}(1,3)$ of lines contained in $X$.

    Observe that if we fix a line $L$ in $\mathbb{P}^3$, then the condition that $L$ lies in $X$ is equivalent to the condition that the restriction of $g$ to $L$ is identically zero, i.e. that $g$ is sent to 0 by the restriction map $H^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(3)) \to H^0(\mathcal{O}_L(3))$. Note that the restriction map from the 20-dimensional vector space of cubic forms on $\mathbb{P}^3$ to the 4-dimensional vector space of cubic forms on $L$, $V_L = H^0(L, \mathcal{O}_L(3))$ is a linear surjection, and the condition $L \subseteq X$ is that $F$ maps to 0 in $V_L$. This imposes four linear conditions on the coefficients of $F$. 

    As the line varies over $\mathbb{G}(1,3)$, the vector spaces $V_L$ fit together to form a vector bundle of rank 4, $\mathcal{V}$ on $\mathbb{G}(1,3)$, with fiber over the point $[L]$ being $V_L$. A cubic form $F$ on $\mathbb{P}^3$, through its restriction to each $V_L$, defines a global section $\sigma_F$ of this vector bundle. Thus, the locus of lines contained in the cubic surface $X$ is the zero locus of the section $\sigma_F$. Assuming that this zero locus is zero-dimensional(!?), its class in $A(\mathbb{G}(1,3))$ is given by the top Chern class $c_4(\mathcal{V})$ of the vector bundle $\mathcal{V}$. The number of lines contained in $X$ is therefore the degree of this class(!?).

    To compute this degree, we first express $\mathcal{V}$ in terms of the universal subbundle $\mathcal{S}$ on $\mathbb{G}(1,3)$. Recall that the fiber of $\mathcal{S}$ over a point $[L]$ is the 2-dimensional vector space corresponding to the line $L \subseteq \mathbb{P}^3$. The restriction of cubic forms on $\mathbb{P}^3$ to $L$ factors through the symmetric third power of this vector space, so we have an isomorphism of vector bundles
    \[\mathcal{V} \cong \mathrm{Sym}^3(\mathcal{S}^\vee).\]
    Using the splitting principle, we can compute the Chern classes of $\mathcal{V}$ in terms of the Chern classes of $\mathcal{S}^\vee$. Let the Chern roots of $\mathcal{S}^\vee$ be $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$. Then the Chern roots of $\mathcal{V}$ are given by
    \[3\alpha_1, 2\alpha_1 + \alpha_2, \alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2, 3\alpha_2.\]
    Thus, the top Chern class of $\mathcal{V}$ is
    \begin{align*}
        c_4(\mathcal{V}) &= (3\alpha_1)(2\alpha_1 + \alpha_2)(\alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2)(3\alpha_2) \\
        &= 9 \alpha_1 \alpha_2 (2\alpha_1^2 + 5\alpha_1 \alpha_2 + 2\alpha_2^2) \\
        &= 9 c_2(\mathcal{S}^\vee) (2 c_1(\mathcal{S}^\vee)^2 + 5 c_2(\mathcal{S}^\vee)).
    \end{align*}
    To compute the degree of this class, we express it in terms of the Schubert classes $\sigma_1 = c_1(\mathcal{S}^\vee)$ and $\sigma_{1,1} = c_2(\mathcal{S}^\vee)$, and use the intersection numbers computed in (!?).
    Therefore, a smooth cubic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ contains exactly 27 distinct lines.
\end{proof}
