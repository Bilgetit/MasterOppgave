From \cref{cor:Fano}, we know that a smooth cubic surface contains finitely many lines. We will now show that it contains exactly 27 lines. \begin{theorem}
    Each smooth cubic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ contains exactly 27 distinct lines.
\end{theorem}

This will be the main theorem of this section. We will prove it in two different ways. The first will be a well known proof, relating cubic surfaces to blow-ups of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in 6 points. This proof will rely on theory which is not in the scope of this thesis, but it will allow us to draw a picture to show a representation of the 27 lines themselves, which we will use later.

The points $P_1, \dots, P_6$ in $\mathbb{P}^2$ are said to be in \emph{general position} if no three of the points are collinear, and no six lie on a conic.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:blowup6}
    (\cite{Ha77}, 4.7/4.7.3) Let $P_1, \dots, P_6 \in \mathbb{P}^2$ be in general position. Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a smooth cubic surface. Then $X$ is isomorphic to the blow-up of $\mathbb{P}^2$ in the points $P_1, \dots, P_6$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:27}
    (\cite{Ha77}, 4.9) Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a smooth cubic surface. Then $X$ contains exactly 27 distinct lines. Each having self-intersection $-1$, and they are the only irreducible curves with negative self-intersection. They are:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The exceptional curves $E_1, \dots E_6$ (6 of these)
        \item The strict transform of lines in $\mathbb{P}^2$ containing pairs of points $P_i, P_j$, $i \neq j$ (15 of these).
        \item The strict transform of conics in $\mathbb{P}^2$ not containing $P_i$ (6 of these)
    \end{itemize}
\end{proposition}
